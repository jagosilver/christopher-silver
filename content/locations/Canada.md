The Canadian photographs were taken in **1961** on Kodachrome colour film — vivid, warm-toned slides that capture the full sweep of a trans-Canada road journey. CPS was 41 that year. The route appears to have taken him and his wife Nancy from the Atlantic coast (Halifax, Nova Scotia) westward through Ontario (Toronto, Niagara) and the prairies (Regina, Saskatchewan) to the Pacific coast of British Columbia, including the Fraser Canyon, the North Thompson River valley, Vancouver, and Victoria. These are among the most visually striking photographs in the collection, benefiting from the exceptional colour fidelity of Kodachrome 1961 film.

### Fraser Canyon

The Fraser Canyon cuts through the Coast Mountains of British Columbia — a dramatic gorge carved by the Fraser River, and a major engineering challenge for the Canadian Pacific Railway and the Trans-Canada Highway.

![CPS and his wife **Nancy** standing at the approach to a suspension bridge over the Fraser Canyon — the steel cable-stayed bridge disappears into the forested canyon behind them. Nancy wears a plaid coat; Christopher is in a dark suit. One of the few photographs in which CPS himself appears. The bright Kodachrome colours capture the autumn light perfectly.](images/CPS Canada Fraser Canyon British Columbia 01.jpg)

## Anahim Lake Stampede

Anahim Lake is a remote community in the Chilcotin plateau of British Columbia. Its annual stampede — a traditional ranch rodeo — draws riders and spectators from across the region.

![The Anahim Lake Stampede: riders on horseback in a rustic log-rail corral arena, spectators perched along the fence rails. Rolling treeless hills rise behind. An authentic, unself-conscious piece of 1961 ranch life in the remote BC interior. *Note: this photograph was grouped with the USA slides in the original collection; geographically it belongs to Canada.*](images/CPS USA Anahim Lake Stampede 01.jpg)
