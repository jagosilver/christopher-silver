[Christopher Patrick Silver](Christopher-Silver) served in India with the Royal Army Medical Corps (RAMC) during the Second World War, arriving after the Italian campaign and remaining until demobilisation in 1946. He held the rank of temporary major. These photographs were almost certainly taken during that period — most likely in Bombay (now Mumbai), India's principal port city and the hub of British military activity in the region. They are vivid documents of late colonial India, capturing street life, transport, and the social spectacle of the Bombay Racecourse.

## Bombay street scenes

## Bombay Racecourse

The Bombay Racecourse (now Mahalaxmi Racecourse) was one of the great social venues of the British Raj — a place where military officers, colonial administrators, wealthy Indian families and the general public gathered together. CPS photographed a race meeting there in considerable detail, capturing both the grandeur of the event and the vast, mixed crowds.

![The parade ring at the Bombay Racecourse. A handsome white colonial building forms the backdrop; horses are being walked around the ring, watched by a large, formally dressed crowd. The scale and elegance of the venue is unmistakable.](../../images/CPS%20India%20Bombay%20Racecourse%2001.jpg)

![Horses leaving the parade ring for the course — a jockey in white silks mounted on a dark horse, led by a groom, passes before a dense crowd lining the hedge. Other horses and riders follow behind. A close, intimate shot.](../../images/CPS%20India%20Bombay%20Racecourse%2002.jpg)

![A panoramic view of the Bombay Racecourse during a race. The main straight is visible, flanked by enormous crowds. The grandstand is packed; industrial chimneys smoke on the skyline beyond the course. The scale of the gathering — many thousands — is extraordinary.](../../images/CPS%20India%20Bombay%20Racecourse%2003.jpg)

## Notes
CPS arrived in India following service in North Africa and Italy. In Italy he broke his leg in a blackout accident and was hospitalised, reportedly witnessing the 1944 eruption of Mount Vesuvius from his hospital bed before being posted onward to India. He was demobilised in 1946 with the rank of temporary major.
